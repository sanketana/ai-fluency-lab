# Example: Interactive Timeline — Indian Independence Movement

**Explainer type:** Timeline
**Subject:** History — Indian Independence Movement (1857-1947)
**Level:** Grade 10 ICSE
**What makes this a good example:** Covers a commonly tested topic, color-coded by category, includes cause-effect connections, and has a quiz mode

---

## Prompt Used

```
Create an interactive timeline as an artifact for:

Topic: Key events of the Indian Independence Movement, 1857-1947
Level: Grade 10 ICSE History

Requirements:
- Create a horizontal scrollable timeline spanning 1857 to 1947
- Include 20 key events that are most important for ICSE board exams
- Color code events by category:
  - 🔴 Red: Major revolts and movements (Quit India, Non-Cooperation, Civil Disobedience)
  - 🔵 Blue: Important legislation and acts (Rowlatt Act, Government of India Act)
  - 🟢 Green: Key organizations and sessions (INC formation, Lahore Session)
  - 🟡 Yellow: Key figures and turning points (Gandhi's arrival, Jallianwala Bagh)
- Each event shows: Year, Event Title, and a 2-sentence description
- Clicking an event expands it to show:
  - What happened (3-4 sentences)
  - Why it matters (significance for the independence movement)
  - Key people involved
  - Connection to the next major event (cause-effect link)
- Include a color-coded legend at the top
- Add connecting arrows between events that directly caused or influenced each other
- At the bottom, include a 4-sentence summary of the overall arc from 1857 to 1947

After the timeline, include a "Test Yourself" mode:
- Show the timeline with event titles hidden
- Give me the event description and I have to place it on the correct year
- Score my accuracy at the end

Make sure all dates, event descriptions, and connections are historically accurate according to the ICSE curriculum.
```

---

## What the Output Looks Like

The artifact generates a scrollable timeline with:
- **Horizontal timeline bar** spanning 1857-1947 with tick marks at key years
- **20 color-coded event markers** placed along the timeline, clustered more densely in the 1919-1947 period
- **Legend** at top: Red = Revolts/Movements, Blue = Legislation, Green = Organizations, Yellow = Turning Points
- **Expandable cards** — clicking "1919: Jallianwala Bagh Massacre" expands to show full details, significance, key figures (General Dyer, impact on public opinion), and an arrow connecting to "1920: Non-Cooperation Movement"
- **Cause-effect arrows** — visible connecting lines between related events (e.g., Rowlatt Act → Jallianwala Bagh → Non-Cooperation Movement)
- **Summary bar** at bottom: "The Indian Independence Movement evolved from the armed revolt of 1857 through decades of organized political resistance..."
- **Quiz mode** — timeline goes blank, event descriptions appear one at a time, student drags them to the correct position

**Key events included:**
1857 Revolt, 1885 INC Formation, 1905 Bengal Partition, 1906 Muslim League, 1909 Morley-Minto Reforms, 1915 Gandhi returns to India, 1919 Rowlatt Act, 1919 Jallianwala Bagh, 1920 Non-Cooperation Movement, 1928 Simon Commission, 1929 Lahore Session (Purna Swaraj), 1930 Dandi March, 1930 Civil Disobedience Movement, 1935 Government of India Act, 1939 Congress ministries resign (WWII), 1942 Quit India Movement, 1943 Azad Hind Fauj, 1946 Cabinet Mission, 1947 Mountbatten Plan, 1947 Independence

**Actions taken:**

2. Updated Azad Hind Fauj entry: "Originally formed in 1942; reorganized under Subhas Chandra Bose in 1943"

## Why This Example Works

1. **Exam-focused selection** — asked for events important for ICSE boards, not a comprehensive encyclopedia
2. **Category system** — color coding makes it easy to see patterns (legislation triggers revolts, organizations enable movements)
3. **Cause-effect arrows** — shows WHY events happened in sequence, not just WHEN — this is what exam questions actually test
4. **Quiz mode** — turns passive reading into active recall, which is proven to improve retention
5. **Caught real issues** — the Dandi March distance and Azad Hind Fauj date corrections are exactly the kind of errors students need to catch before an exam
